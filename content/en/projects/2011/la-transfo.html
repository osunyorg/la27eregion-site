Simulating the existence of an innovation laboratory within a dozen local authorities (2011–2020)

body: >-

Summary

La Transfo is a participatory action research programme carried out between 2011 and 2020 with eleven local authorities. It aimed to explore how to create and then sustain an innovation lab within each of these local authorities. To this end, a multidisciplinary team of in-residence experts (designers, sociologists, urban planners, etc.) was embedded within each partner local authority for 18 to 24 months. Their role was to work hand in hand with a group of volunteer ‘ambassador’ staff to test, in real-world situations and through very concrete practical cases, new ways of designing or improving existing systems, and to draw lessons from this regarding the functioning of the future innovation laboratory (its methods, its team, its governance, etc.). La Transfo was designed as an open framework enabling the creation of knowledge across local authorities, through days of reflection, discussion and analysis that brought together the teams involved in the programme.

What was at stake ?

More and more local authorities are seeking new approaches and solutions to tackle complex challenges in the fields of housing policy, poverty alleviation, public health, security and the environment... Transfo's hypothesis is that public innovation labs can play a role at several levels:

At the administrative level : designing and implementing public policies through a process that starts with the heart of the problem and allows for prototyping and testing prior to implementation - an approach that generates greater impact and transcends organisational silos.

At the level of individual staff members : fostering engagement and well-being at work, empathy towards service users and colleagues, a sense of creativity and ingenuity, and the ability to question one's professional routines.

At the political level: making elected representatives more attentive to the processes of design and implementation, as well as to the actual impacts of public policies, and facilitating cooperation with the administration.

Timeline

2011-2014: 4 pilot regions
From 2011 to 2014, the Transfo programme was run in partnership with four pilot regions: Burgundy, Champagne-Ardenne, Pays de la Loire and Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur. It enabled three of these regions to set up their own labs, which have since independently carried out dozens of projects on a variety of topics and continue to exchange ideas regularly with one another. In 2014, La Transfo won awards at the Victoires des Acteurs Publics and Design Management Europe.

2016-2019: 7 new local authorities
A second phase was launched in 2016 with the aim of engaging 7 new local authorities over 4 years: the City of Paris, the Dunkirk Urban Community, the Strasbourg Eurometropolis, the Metz Metropolis, the City of Mulhouse, the Lille European Metropolis and the Occitanie Region.

2020: Post-Transfo
The  Post Transfo  phase (June 2019-autumn 2020) aimed to ensure the sustainability and/or structuring of the 'innovation' functions of 10 local authorities or groups of local authorities (Grenoble Metropolis, Nantes Metropolis, etc.), whilst also helping them to take their approach a step further by providing a neutral space in which to explore new approaches, take risks, and draw inspiration from peers in France and abroad.

Approach

La phase Post Transfo (juin 2019 - automne 2020) visait à garantir la pérennité et/ou la structuration des fonctions « d'innovation » de 10 collectivités territoriales ou groupements de collectivités territoriales (Métropole de Grenoble, Métropole de Nantes, etc.), tout en les aidant à faire évoluer leur démarche grâce à un espace neutre leur permettant d'explorer de nouvelles approches, de prendre des risques et de s'inspirer de leurs pairs en France et à l'étranger.
