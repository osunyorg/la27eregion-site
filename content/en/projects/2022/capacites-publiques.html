Strengthening the resilience of public administrations in the face of crises (2022)

body: >-

Context

Following on from the Réflexes publics i nvestigation on innovations developed within public administrations at the start of the pandemic in 2020, the Capacités Publiques programme aimed to lay the groundwork, through practical experience, for the resilient public administration of the future, by experimenting with three areas: supporting spontaneous initiatives, professional micro-mobility and the management of subsidiarity. These areas serve as levers for action to ensure that public sector transformation can effectively reveal and strengthen the capacity of organisations and local authorities to respond, bounce back and mitigate the impact of crises, whilst also better addressing the more chronic or structural needs of public administrations and their local partners.

Approach
Shared experimental capacity
From January 2022 to December 2022, Capacités Publiques engaged 12 public organisations in a process that alternated between group working sessions and peer-to-peer knowledge-sharing to develop pilot schemes, and the testing, within each public body, of practical applications tailored to each participant’s organisation and local context. The programme was documented on a dedicated blog and in a final report, and a mirror group was set up to facilitate the dissemination of the results of the pilot schemes to all public sector stakeholders.

Active involvement of organisations
The programme was based on the principle of active participation by the partner organisations: two to three staff members from each organisation (sometimes more) took part in the group sessions and led the trials carried out within their organisations by involving colleagues from various departments.

Method : 3 ideas to experiment
Idea 1: Supporting grassroots initiatives
The pandemic has led to numerous instances of staff carrying out actions that were sometimes essential to crisis management, without always having been given the authority to do so. Despite a consensus on the need to act and, with hindsight, on the positive impact of their involvement, there can be a significant lack of understanding between the ‘lone hero’ and their line managers. So how can we recognise and value this process of bottom-up innovation through a framework that encourages initiative and considers the (re)integration – of both initiatives and staff members – within the local authority?

Idea 2: Professional micro-mobility
The redeployment of staff has been a recurring feature of crisis management, taking various forms and involving different methods of implementation, but always with the same aim: to reallocate staff to essential services and immediate priorities. So how can we develop a new HR culture and a mobility policy based on valuing versatility, the ability to adapt to changing circumstances, and solidarity between departments and between stakeholders within the same region?

Idea 3: subsidiarity management
The crisis has also given rise to professional practices that grant staff and managers greater autonomy: the duty to take the initiative, the freedom to adapt rules, a reduction in reporting ‘noise’, and the streamlining of approval processes… So how can we draw on the lessons of the crisis to develop a more horizontal approach to public management, one which, in particular, recognises that all managers play a key role as cogs in the machinery of public action, by increasing their capacity to act and reinforcing their professional identity?

Programme phases
JANUARY TO MARCH 2022: COLLABORATIVE EXPLORATORY SESSIONS
– To document the theme, drawing on the contexts and experiences within the participating organisations, not only during the first lockdown but also before and after it, and to set out initial ideas for future trials. – Share inspiring experiences relating to the theme and develop the initial ideas for the trials further. – Finalise the broad outlines of the trials to be carried out within the various organisations and map out the key stages, actions and procedures for communication, documentation and evaluation over time.

MAY TO NOVEMBER 2022: PREPARATION AND IMPLEMENTATION OF TESTS
- Preparation of tests: defining the precise scope and stages of the tests, identifying and mobilising the testing teams, defining evaluation criteria. - Implementing 'independent' tests within each government department, supported by La 27e Région's 'experimentation hotline': regular updates, provision of resources and inspiration, involvement at key stages, and ad hoc mobilisation of expertise (management/HR, design, etc.).

JUNE 2022
A joint workshop in Paris to discuss the progress of discussions and testing.
